# Top-Journal Benchmark For Scenario Experiments

Use this reference when designing behavioral scenario experiments for management, marketing, information systems, consumer psychology, applied psychology, and related fields.

## Ranking Lists To Treat Carefully

- UTD Top 100 Business School Research Rankings use publications in 24 top-tier peer-reviewed business journals; the 2026 page states that the database was created in 2005 and tracks publications in those journals since 1990.
- FT50 refers to the 50 journals used by the Financial Times research rank; the FT list was reviewed in 2016 and expanded from 45 to 50.
- Chartered ABS Academic Journal Guide, often called AJG or ABS, updates periodically; verify the exact target-year rating before claiming that a journal is rated 4 or 4*.
- APA JARS-Quant and AGReMA are useful reporting references for quantitative experiments and mediation analyses.

Do not use UTD24, FT50, or ABS4 as a proxy for method quality by itself. Use them as publication-context benchmarks.

Useful official/reference sources to verify before making ranking or reporting claims:

- UTD Top 100 Business School Research Rankings, UT Dallas Jindal School: https://jsom.utdallas.edu/the-utd-top-100-business-school-research-rankings/?t=ad

## What Top Journals Usually Expect

### Contribution

- A mechanism that explains why the manipulated situation changes the outcome.
- A boundary condition that matters theoretically, not just statistically.
- A multi-study logic when the claim is broad.
- Clear connection between experimental abstraction and real-world phenomenon.

### Design

- Clean manipulation with minimal confounds.
- Pretest or pilot for manipulation validity.
- Random assignment and explicit cell sizes.
- Attention, realism, and suspicion checks when appropriate.
- Behavioral or consequential outcome when possible.

### Analysis

- Exact p-values, standard errors or confidence intervals, and effect sizes.
- Clear distinction between confirmatory and exploratory results.
- Mediation reported as indirect effect with uncertainty interval, not only path significance.
- Interaction interpreted with simple effects or marginal effects, not only the interaction coefficient.

### Reporting

- Materials and measures in appendix.
- Data/code availability statement when journal policy requires it.
- Pre-registration statement if available.
- Transparent exclusions.

## Cross-Journal Design Patterns

| Journal family | Stronger design signal | Weak design signal |
|---|---|---|
| JCR/JCP | Psychological mechanism, construct-valid manipulation, multiple converging studies | One intuitive main effect with no mechanism |
| JM/JMR | Substantive marketing problem, managerially meaningful effect, clean causal identification | Scenario is interesting but detached from market behavior |
| JAP/OBHDP | Validated measures, clear work/decision context, strong internal validity | Workplace label pasted onto a generic vignette |
| ISR/MISQ | Realistic technology interaction, system affordance clarity, user-task fit | AI/system manipulation confounded with novelty, quality, privacy, or difficulty |
| JPSP/Psychological Science | Precise psychological process, high-power design, transparent materials | Broad psychological claim from a small one-shot vignette |

## Common Desk-Rejection Risks

- Scenario manipulation is obvious, artificial, or confounded.
- Study only shows an intuitive main effect with no theoretical mechanism.
- Manipulation check is treated as evidence for the hypothesis.
- Mediator is just a reworded DV.
- Claims exceed what a one-shot online scenario can support.
- No evidence that participants understood or believed the scenario.
