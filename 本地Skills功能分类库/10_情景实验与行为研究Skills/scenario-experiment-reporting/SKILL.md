# Scenario Experiment Reporting

Use this skill to write, revise, or audit Methods, Results, appendices, and reviewer-facing explanations for scenario experiments.

## Reporting Standard

Use `references/reporting-templates.md` for ready-to-use templates.

Use `references/top-journal-benchmark.md` when the user wants a specific journal family.

Follow these principles:

- Describe the scenario, manipulation, random assignment, sample, exclusions, and measures clearly enough for replication.
- Report exact p-values, standard errors or confidence intervals, and effect sizes.
- Distinguish manipulation checks, primary tests, mechanism tests, robustness checks, and exploratory analyses.
- Do not overstate scenario experiments as field behavior unless the DV is behavioral.
- Do not call a result causal if randomization, manipulation validity, or exclusion rules are weak.

## Method Section Template

Write in this order:

1. Purpose of the experiment.
2. Design and conditions.
3. Participants, recruitment, and exclusions.
4. Procedure and random assignment.
5. Scenario stimuli.
6. Measures and anchors.
7. Manipulation, attention, realism, and confound checks.
8. Analysis plan.

## Results Section Template

Write in this order:

1. Exclusion and final sample.
2. Randomization check if relevant.
3. Manipulation check.
4. Reliability.
5. Primary test.
6. Mediation or moderation.
7. Robustness.
8. Interpretation in theory terms.

## Table Requirements

Preferred tables:

- Table 1: design, sample, exclusions, cells.
- Table 2: descriptive statistics and correlations.
- Table 3: primary regression or ANOVA model.
- Table 4: mediation/moderation/conditional effects.
- Appendix table: measures and stimuli.

Tables should include:

- Estimate or mean difference.
- Standard error or confidence interval.
- Exact p-value.
- Effect size.
- N per condition.

## Figure Requirements

Use figures for:

- 2 x 2 interactions.
- Mediation model path diagram.
- Conditional indirect effects.
- Cell means with confidence intervals.

Do not use bar charts without uncertainty intervals. Prefer point-range plots for condition means.

## Reviewer Audit

Before finalizing, check:

- Is the manipulation theoretically tied to the construct?
- Are confounds addressed?
- Are all exclusions transparent?
- Are manipulation checks separated from hypothesis tests?
- Does the mediation claim meet temporal and conceptual plausibility?
- Does the text report effect magnitude, not just p-values?
- Are materials, data, code, and preregistration described?

## Tone

Use concise academic prose. Avoid inflated claims such as "fully proves", "definitively demonstrates", or "validates the theory". Prefer "provides evidence consistent with".
